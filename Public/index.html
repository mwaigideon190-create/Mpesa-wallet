Trend Followingdirectional

Find the prevailing direction using moving averages or higher highs and higher lows, and trade with it until signs of reversal appear.

Main risk

Choppy, sideways markets can trigger repeated false entries.

Breakout Tradingmomentum

Enter when price clears a defined support or resistance level on rising volume, expecting continuation.

Main risk

Price can break a level and quickly return inside the range.

Moving Average Crossovertrend

With two averages such as 50 and 200 period, the shorter crossing above the longer signals strength, and crossing below signals weakness.

Main risk

Averages lag price, so crossovers often arrive after much of the move.

Range Tradingsideways

In a sideways market, buy near support and sell near resistance while the range holds.

Main risk

A range can end suddenly and turn into a strong trend.

RSI Mean Reversionreversal

RSI above 70 flags overbought, below 30 flags oversold. The idea is that price drifts back toward its average.

Main risk

In a strong trend, RSI can stay overbought or oversold for a long time.

Scalpingshort-term

Many quick, small trades held seconds to minutes, which depends on tight spreads and high liquidity.

Main risk

Spreads and fees add up and can outweigh small gains.

Swing Tradingmedium-term

Hold for days to weeks to capture a larger swing, combining technical setups with the wider trend.

Main risk

Positions held overnight or over weekends can gap past a stop-loss.

News Tradingevent-driven

Trade around major releases like rate decisions and jobs data, where volatility often spikes.

Main risk

Spreads widen and orders can fill far from the price you expected.

Risk Managementdiscipline

Many disciplined traders risk only 1 to 2 percent of capital per trade and always use a stop-loss.

Main risk

In fast markets a stop-loss can fill worse than its set price.
